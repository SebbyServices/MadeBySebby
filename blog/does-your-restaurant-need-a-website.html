Does Your Restaurant Need a Website?

You have an Instagram page. You're on Google Maps. You get foot traffic. So why would you spend money on a website?

Because the restaurants that are growing right now aren't relying on one channel. They're showing up everywhere their customers look. And a website is the only piece of digital real estate you actually own.

People Search Before They Eat

Think about the last time you tried a new restaurant. You probably didn't walk in off the street. You looked it up first. You checked the menu. You scrolled through photos. You looked at the hours. Maybe you checked if they take reservations.

Your customers do the same thing. According to a 2024 TouchBistro report, 77% of diners visit a restaurant's website before deciding where to eat. Not their Instagram. Not their Google listing. Their website.

When they don't find a website, one of two things happens. They go somewhere else, or they make do with whatever outdated info Google scraped together. Either way, you're losing control of the experience before the customer even walks through your door.

Instagram Is Not a Website

This is the most common thing I hear from restaurant owners: "I don't need a website, I have Instagram." And look, Instagram is a powerful tool. Great for showcasing food, building a following, running promotions. But it has real limitations that hurt you.

You can't put a menu on Instagram. Not a real one. Sure, you can post a photo of a menu, but it's hard to read on a phone, impossible to update section by section, and it doesn't show up when someone Googles "your restaurant name + menu." A website with a clean, text-based menu page ranks on Google and takes two seconds to read.

Instagram doesn't show your hours or location clearly. It's buried in your bio, if it's there at all. Your website puts your address, hours, phone number, and a map front and center. That's what people are actually looking for.

You don't own your Instagram audience. If Instagram changes its algorithm tomorrow, your reach drops. If your account gets flagged or hacked, your audience is gone. Your website is yours. No algorithm decides who sees it.

What a Restaurant Website Actually Needs

Restaurant websites don't need to be complicated. They need to answer the five questions every hungry person has. Get these right and your website is already better than 90% of what's out there.

1. What do you serve? A text-based menu that's easy to read on a phone. Not a PDF. Not a photo of a paper menu. Clean text with prices, organized by category. Bonus: it helps Google understand what you serve, which means better search rankings for things like "sushi near me" or "Dominican food Santo Domingo."

2. Where are you? Your address, embedded Google Maps, and clear driving or walking directions. If you have parking, say so. If you're in a tricky location, explain how to find you. This sounds obvious but most restaurant websites either skip it or make you dig for it.

3. When are you open? Your hours, updated when they change. Holiday hours, special closings, seasonal schedules. If someone drives across town because Google says you're open and you're not, you lost that customer forever.

4. Can I make a reservation or order online? Even if you use a third-party tool like OpenTable, Resy, or a WhatsApp link, your website is the best place to put it. One clear button. No hunting, no guessing. According to the National Restaurant Association, 67% of restaurant customers have ordered food online in the past year. If your website doesn't make that easy, someone else's will.

5. What's the vibe? A few good photos of the space, the food, and the people. Not stock photos. Real images that show what walking in feels like. This builds trust and sets expectations. People want to know what they're walking into before they get there.

Google Can't Read Your Instagram Posts

Here's the thing most restaurant owners don't realize: Google ranks websites, not Instagram accounts. When someone searches "best seafood in Santo Domingo" or "Italian restaurant near me," Google is looking at websites to decide who shows up first.

If you don't have a website, you're depending entirely on your Google Business Profile. And while that's important, it's limited. You can't control the full story. You can't add a menu page optimized for "brunch menu Miami." You can't write a description that targets "family restaurant with outdoor seating." A website lets you show up for specific searches that your Google listing alone can't win.

This matters even more in markets like Santo Domingo, where most restaurants have no website at all. The bar is low. A simple, well-built website immediately sets you apart from every competitor who's still relying on Instagram and word of mouth alone.

The Cost Is Lower Than You Think

Most restaurant owners overestimate what a website costs. You don't need a custom app. You don't need online ordering built from scratch. You don't need a $10,000 agency project.

A solid restaurant website with your menu, location, hours, photos, and a reservation or ordering link can be built for a fraction of what most people expect. The question isn't whether you can afford a website. It's whether you can afford not to have one while your competitors do.

And unlike a social media ad that stops working the moment you stop paying, a website keeps working for you 24/7. It shows up in Google searches at 2 AM when someone is deciding where to eat tomorrow. It answers questions while you're cooking. It builds trust while you're sleeping.

What to Do Right Now

If you don't have a website, start with the basics. You need five things and nothing more:

A clean homepage that says who you are and what you serve in the first three seconds. A menu page with readable text and current prices. A location and hours page with Google Maps embedded. A contact page with your phone number, email, and a WhatsApp link if you use it. Photos that show the real food and the real space.

That's it. You can always add more later. But those five pages put you ahead of every restaurant in your area that doesn't have a website, which in many markets is most of them.
