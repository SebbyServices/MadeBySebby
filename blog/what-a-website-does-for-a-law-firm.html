What a Website Actually Does for a Law Firm

You passed the bar. You built a practice. You earned a reputation in your community. But when someone in your city types "lawyer near me" into Google, are they finding you or your competitor down the street?

I built a law firm website from the ground up for a securities arbitration attorney in Coral Gables, Florida. The project taught me exactly what works, what lawyers overlook, and what their clients actually need to see before they pick up the phone. This post is everything I learned, backed by data.

The data: your next client is Googling you right now

Let's skip the opinions and look at the numbers.

87% of consumers say they would use Google to research a lawyer.

Source: iLawyerMarketing Consumer Study, 2025

Google is still the dominant starting point. No other channel comes close: in the same 2025 study, 28% named ChatGPT, 25% Facebook and 24% Yelp.

If your firm is not showing up in those results, you are invisible to the majority of potential clients.

And it is not just Google anymore. 28% of consumers said they would use ChatGPT to research a lawyer in 2025, up from just 9% in 2023 (iLawyerMarketing). AI search is growing fast. But here is the key detail: 94% of those people also use Google. AI is additive. It is not a replacement. Your website still matters.

What clients actually look for on a law firm website

People hiring a lawyer are not browsing casually. They have a problem. They are stressed. They want to know three things fast:

  • Can this person handle my case? They want to see specific practice areas, not vague language.
  • Is this person credible? They look for real attorney photos, credentials, bar memberships, and case results.
  • Can I trust this person? They read testimonials and reviews. Half of consumers trust online reviews as much as a personal recommendation from friends or family (BrightLocal, 2024).

The law firm websites that pull their weight lean on the same trust signals: client testimonials, professional certifications, bar association memberships, case results, and industry recognition. Visitors make a judgment about your firm in the first few seconds of landing on your site. If they do not see credibility immediately, they leave.

5 things every law firm website needs

1. Clear practice area pages

One page per specialty. Not a dropdown menu that lists 14 practice areas with a single paragraph each. A dedicated page that explains what you do, who you help, and what the process looks like. This is what Google indexes. This is what clients read. If you handle personal injury, securities fraud, and family law, each one needs its own page with real content.

2. Attorney bios with real photos

Stock photos erode trust instantly. Your bio page should include a professional headshot, your education, bar admissions, notable cases or experience, and a few sentences that sound like a human wrote them. People hire people, not firms. Let them see who they are calling.

3. Client testimonials and case results

Testimonials on your own site carry weight, especially when they include specific details about the type of case and outcome. If your state bar allows it, include settlement amounts or verdict results. If not, focus on the client experience.

A note on ethics: ABA Model Rule 7.1 requires that all communications about a lawyer's services be truthful and not misleading. That includes testimonials and case results on your website. State rules vary, so check with your bar before publishing specific outcomes. States like Florida, New York, and Texas have stricter advertising requirements than others.

4. Mobile-responsive design

According to Statcounter, phones accounted for 53% of the world's web traffic in July 2026, with the United States close to an even split. Legal searches skew to the phone even more, because people look for a lawyer at the moment the problem lands, and that is rarely at a desk. If your site does not load fast and look clean on a phone screen, you are losing a large share of your visitors before they read a word.

5. Easy contact: click-to-call, forms, and booking

Your website needs a visible phone number on every page (click-to-call on mobile), a contact form that works, and ideally a link to book a consultation directly. Every extra click between a visitor and your inbox is a lost lead.

Real example: Riera Law Firm

I did not write this post from theory. I built a website for Riera Law Firm, a securities arbitration practice led by a former SEC enforcement attorney in Coral Gables, Florida.

When I started, the firm had two competing websites on the same domain causing DNS conflicts, broken email delivery (messages from clients and courts were going missing), plugin security vulnerabilities, and no Spanish-language presence despite serving a bilingual client base.

I rebuilt the entire site from scratch: 70+ pages covering every practice area, full bilingual support in English and Spanish, proper DNS and email restoration, a custom design system that reflects the authority of a federal-level practice, and an ongoing monthly care plan for security updates and maintenance.

The result? Zero downtime during migration. A site that earns trust from the first second. And a client who said: "He treats my site like it's his own. I trust him with the online face of my practice." You can read the full case study here.

What it costs (honest answer)

Most custom law firm websites run between $2,500 and $9,000 USD. That range gets you a professionally designed, mobile-responsive site with dedicated practice area pages, attorney bios, contact forms, and basic SEO. Larger firms or sites with complex features like client portals or case result databases can run higher.

The cheap alternatives exist. You can get a template site for $500 or use a DIY builder for $20 a month. But consider what it says to a prospective client when your website looks like every other template on the internet. You did not take a shortcut on your law degree. Your website should not look like you did either.

The referral myth: "All my clients come from referrals"

This is the most common objection I hear from lawyers. "I don't need a website. All my clients come from referrals."

Here is what the data actually says:

46% of people who receive a personal referral to a lawyer still read the lawyer's reviews before making contact, and 45% check the firm's website.

Source: Martindale-Avvo, Hiring an Attorney, 2019

Nearly half of your referrals are Googling you before they call. They are looking at your website, reading your reviews, and deciding whether to follow through on that recommendation. If your site looks outdated, has broken links, or does not clearly show what you do, you are losing people who were already warm leads.

Over 75% of clients visit two to five law firm websites before deciding who to contact (FindLaw Consumer Legal Needs Survey, 2023). Your website is not just a digital business card. It is the deciding factor in whether someone calls you or the next firm on the list.

Speed matters more than you think

This one surprises most attorneys. It is not just about having a website. It is about how fast you respond once someone reaches out through it.

79% of clients expect a law firm to respond within 24 hours (Clio Legal Trends Report). That means if someone fills out your contact form on a Friday evening and you do not get back to them until Monday, there is a strong chance they have already hired someone else.

A good law firm website makes this easier by connecting form submissions to your email or practice management software instantly, offering click-to-call on mobile, and even providing a direct booking link so clients can schedule a consultation without waiting for a callback.

Bottom line

A law firm website is not a brochure. It is the front door to your practice. Most of your future clients will walk through it before they ever pick up the phone. It needs to load fast, look professional, clearly communicate your expertise, and make it effortless to get in touch.
